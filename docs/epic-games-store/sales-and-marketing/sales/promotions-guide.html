Promotions Quick Guide

How to use promotional campaigns to publicize your product.

6 mins to read

In sales and marketing, publishers use the term "SKU" to mean an item that you sell or make available for free. In this guide, we use the term "SKU" in the same way for items on the Epic Games Store. In the Developer Portal, a SKU is called an offer.

Epic Games Store Campaign Types

You can use the following campaign types to promote your product on the Epic Games Store:

  • Preset campaign: A campaign run by the Epic Games Store. Preset campaigns are associated with specific sales or events, such as the annual Epic Games MEGA Sale. Enroll your SKUs into preset campaigns to take advantage of the promotional boosts that these campaigns provide. Preset campaigns have fixed names and fixed start and end dates, which are set by the Epic Games Store. To enroll your SKUs in a preset campaign, you create a discount, select the preset campaign as the discount type, then select the SKUs that you want to enroll in the campaign. (For information on how to create a discount, see documentation: Manage Discounts.)

    Throughout the year, the Epic Games Store runs the following preset campaigns:

    • Tentpole Campaign: Epic Games' branded sale campaigns that happen twice a year: the MEGA Sale and the Holiday Sale. These drive millions of players to the Epic Games Store looking to engage with products that take part in the campaigns.
    • Seasonal Campaign: Seasonal sales that are highly anticipated by Epic Games Store users. They align with worldwide holidays and events, including the Lunar New Year, Spring Sale, Summer Sale, Autumn Sale, and Halloween Sale campaigns.
    • Flex Campaign: Our Flex Campaigns–called Epic Savings– take place during price cooldown windows between the tentpole and seasonal sale campaigns. Flex campaigns are more frequent than big campaigns, and are more appropriate for some types of content. To maximize your sales performance, compare how your catalog performs in flex campaigns compared to the big tentpole and seasonal sales.
    • Franchise Event: Franchise Events give a storefront experience that aligns with real-world events, such as May the 4th and Skulls Festival. Note: You can only participate in a franchise event if your content matches the event’s theme.
    • Evergreen Campaign (Deals of the Week): This campaign experience refreshes with new products every Tuesday. The Epic Games Store promotes this campaign on the Epic Games Store Homepage (store.epicgames.com) and on the Epic Games Sales & Specials page (store.epicgames.com/~/sales-and-specials).

  • Custom discount: A campaign that is defined entirely by you, for your own SKUs. Custom discounts are a great opportunity to run full catalog sales and franchise sales to engage with the part of your player base that is looking to buy your content on discount. You can set all the parameters of a custom discount, including the start and end dates, to determine when the discount is available on the storefront.

Note:

  • When you take part in certain preset campaigns, you might not be eligible to join later campaigns, in line with our cooldown period policy. Make sure you are familiar with these guidelines before you take part in a preset campaign. Documentation: Manage Discounts–Discount Guidelines.
  • Preset campaigns on the Epic Games Store usually start and end at 11.00 am ET / 8.00 am PT / 5.00 pm CET / 4.00 pm BST / 3.00 pm UTC.

Discounts

For information, see documentation: Discounts.

Introductory Offers

An introductory offer is a savings claim applied to a SKU at the time of its launch, such as a launch-day sale. A savings claim is a price that is lower than the full price of the product, specified as a percentage of the full price. Introductory offers can run for up to 14 consecutive days.

When you create an introductory offer, you must follow these criteria:

  • The start date and time of the discount must exactly match the SKU's available date and time.
  • The savings claim amount must be between 5% and 40%.
  • The introductory offer duration must be 14 days or less.

For information about how to set up an introductory offer, see documentation: Manage Discounts > Create Introductory Offer.

Note: After your introductory offer ends, you must leave the SKU at the full price for a minimum of 30 days before you can sunset the product or give it away for free.

Pre-Purchase Offers

A pre-purchase offer is a savings claim applied to a SKU during the pre-purchase period. A savings claim is a price that is lower than the full price of the product, specified as a percentage of the full price. The SKU must revert to full price at the available date of the product.

The following guidelines apply to pre-purchase offers:

  • Pre-purchase is only for products that are not yet launched.
  • In the Discount Management Tool, you must set a start date and time on the pre-purchase offer that is exactly the same as the start date and time that you set on the pre-purchase SKU.
  • The pre-purchase offer must end when the product launches.
  • The savings claim amount must be between 5% and 40%.

To learn more about how to submit a pre-purchase offer in the Discount Management Tool, see documentation: Pre-Purchase Offer.

Combine a Pre-Purchase Offer and an Introductory Offer (Special Offers)

If a product has both a pre-purchase offer and an introductory offer, this combination is called a special offer.

The following guidelines apply to special offers:

  • The savings claim amount must be between 5% and 95%. A savings claim is a price that is lower than the full price of the product, specified as a percentage of the full price.
  • The introductory offer must have the same savings claim amount as the pre-purchase offer. For example, if the savings claim amount on the pre-purchase offer is 50, then the savings claim amount on the introductory offer must also be 50.
  • You must submit the introductory offer for review before you submit the pre-purchase offer.

Discounts on Time-Limited SKUs

When you have a SKU that you make available for a limited time, the following guidelines apply for discounts on the SKU:

  • If the SKU has been unavailable on the Epic Games Store for 30 days or less:
    • You can reactivate the SKU and run discounts on the day that you relaunch the SKU.
    • After the discount ends, you must leave the SKU price the same for at least 30 days until you can make the SKU unavailable again.
  • If the SKU has been unavailable on the Epic Games Store store for more than 30 days:
    • When you make the SKU available on the Epic Games Store again, you must leave the SKU at the same price for at least 30 days before you apply a discount. Alternatively, you can do one of the following:
      • Run an introductory offer and then leave the SKU on the Epic Games Store for at least 30 days after the introductory offer ends. More information: Introductory Offers on this page.
      • Set the permanent price of the SKU to the intended discount rate. In this situation, you do not need to leave the price the same for a specific period of time.

Discounts on Bundles

A bundle is an offer that has multiple entitlements.

If you discount a bundle, the discount does not apply to the included entitlements as well. If you want to discount the included entitlements as well, then you must set up discounts for each of the included entitlements separately.

Cooldown Periods on Bundles

When a SKU is part of certain preset campaigns, a cooldown period might apply. This means that your SKU might not be eligible to take part in later campaigns until the cooldown period is over. For more information on cooldown periods, see documentation: Manage Discounts–Discount Guidelines.

If you discount individual entitlements in a bundle, a cooldown period does not apply to the bundle itself. This is because each entitlement in a bundle is treated as an individual product for the purposes of pricing restrictions. This means that you can discount a bundle during different time periods–or the same time periods–as the individual entitlements that are included in it.
